# The Damizza Seat

*A partnership proposal: part of the business, built on his way.*

---

## Why him, why this

In his July 2026 Barrett Media interview, Damizza said the thesis this
product was already built on:

> "Radio didn't lose because of Spotify. Radio lost because it stopped
> being radio."

> "Radio wins when it feels local, spontaneous, and human."

> "Build something people remember — not something they scroll past."

SWELL RADIO is that argument, running as software. Every mechanic in the
app is one of his principles made executable. He shouldn't endorse this
product — he should **own part of it and run the part he invented**.

## His way → already in the product

| Damizza's principle | Shipped mechanic |
|---|---|
| "Stopped being radio" — no more moments | One always-on stream; everyone hears the same second; no skip, no rewind. The ambush is back. |
| Spontaneous and human, not voice-tracked automation | Live crowd programming: boosts/buries shape rotation in real time; nothing is pre-scheduled. |
| Moments people discuss next-day (the Power 106 model) | Vote-driven rotation surges, station events (Top 8 at 8 — roadmap), first-spin premieres (roadmap). |
| Local relevance | The station lives somewhere: live local clock on the dial, and the crowd *is* the locality. |
| Credibility > algorithms | Anti-gaming vote trust earned only by real listening — a crowd that can't be botted is a chart that means something. |
| Catalogs > viral moments | Built on OneSync's owned, opt-in catalog. Artists keep ownership; the station builds eras, not spikes. |

## The seat itself

**Title:** Program Director & Partner (the PD chair, real powers, not a
courtesy credit).

What the PD seat controls in-product (phased):

1. **Now** — PD vote weight: his boost carries founding-level trust from
   day one (already live in the build).
2. **Next** — The premiere lever: PD schedules a track's *first spin*
   (world-premiere culture — his invention at Power). PD cuts station
   drops in his own voice; the station speaks between records.
3. **Then** — Dayparts and station launches: the PD designs the Late
   Shift, signs new stations onto the platform, sets rotation doctrine
   per station.

**The business logic:** he brings the doctrine, the credibility, the
relationships (his stated edge: "reputation opens doors consultants and
data cannot"), and the programming craft. OneSync brings the catalog,
the licensing architecture, and the product.

## Equity — structures to discuss with counsel

*(Standard structures, not legal or financial advice — paper this with
an attorney before any conversation gets specific.)*

- **Advisor-plus grant:** equity vesting over 2–4 years tied to the PD
  role, sized well above the standard advisor band given marquee,
  operating involvement.
- **Co-founder-of-programming:** larger stake with a longer vest and
  defined role scope, if he's in the building, not advising from
  outside.
- **Station partnership overlay:** revenue share on stations he
  programs/launches, stacked on either structure above.

Vesting with a cliff protects both sides; role-based (not time-only)
milestones fit a partner whose contribution is craft and doors opened.

## The handoff moment

Don't send a deck. Send the TestFlight invite and one line:

*"You said radio lost when it stopped being radio. I built one that
never stopped — the flagship sits at 105.9, and the PD chair is empty.
It's yours, and so is a piece of the company."*

Then let him open the app: live station, the crowd programming it, his
frequency on the dial. The product is the pitch.
